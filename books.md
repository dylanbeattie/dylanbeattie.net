# Books

Not books I wrote. I haven't written any books. But once in a while folks ask "can anybody recommend some good books?", so here's a list of authors - and books - that I think are good, and why I think they're good. In alphabetical order by author name, with books in roughly the order I think you should read them.

It probably bears repeating that if you've already read *most* of the books on this page, you'll enjoy the ones you haven't, and if you've read a handful of them and not liked them much, then you probably won't like the others.

## Science Fiction

### Alastair Reynolds:

* **Terminal World**: everything from nanotechnology to steam trains and cowboys in what starts out as a detective story and goes... all kinds of places. I loved it.
* **Revenger** / **Shadow Captain** / **Bone Silence**: The Ness sisters run away from home, become space pirates and search the relics of long-gone civilisations for treasure. Written for young adults, which just means it doesn't have any swears or naughty bits in it; the ideas are every bit as mind-melting as the best of Reynolds' hard SF.
* **Revelation Space**: Thrilling, chilling, galaxy-spanning hard science fiction; while archaeologists untangle the fate of dead alien civilisations, the crew of the lighthugger *Nostalgia for Inifinity* travel the galaxy at relativistic speeds while the Melding Plague infects humans and machines alike with grotesque nanotech.

Those are the highlights. I've not read a Reynolds book I didn't enjoy.

### Stephen Baxter

Sci-fi so hard it'll make your brain hurt. His masterwork is the Xeelee sequence, nine novels and countless short stories 

* **Raft**: what if gravity was so strong that asteroids were habitable and you could throw a spear into around your own body? 
* **Flux**: what if there was sentient life in the magnetic fields inside a neutron star?
